The doctrine

Four things Actaira does not do, and the reason it exists

A compliance tool that holds opinions is useless as proof. These four refusals are invariants of the code, not promises on a page: each one has a gate that fails if it breaks.

Refusal 1

Never a number

There is no such thing as «87% compliant» and none will be issued. What gets published are counts of obligations, never proportions. A test walks every generated document and fails if a percentage shows up.

Refusal 2

Never judge, only cite

Seeing verify_signature in the code is seeing a pattern, not knowing that a measure is in place. Every finding publishes the rule, its version, its pack and its author. The interpretation is signed by a person.

Refusal 3

Never infer the unobserved

An INDETERMINADO with no written reason is a NO_CUMPLE in disguise, and the data model forbids it: an indeterminate result with no named cause blows up on construction.

Refusal 4

Never act on the observed

Actaira says what has to be looked at again. Whether that blocks a deployment is your call, in your CI file, where the decision gets reviewed. The template ships that gate commented out, and there is a test that checks it stays commented out.

The questionnaire

A consultancy opens with three hundred questions because it has not looked at your code

Every question closes both frameworks at once, and disappears when a control reads the bytes that answer it.

«Who is accountable for the management system meeting its requirements, and who reports its performance to top management?» closes Article 16, Article 17, clause 5.3 and control A.3.2 of ISO 42001. You answer once.

And the subtraction is published with the identifiers of the controls that produced it, so it can be audited: a number saying «we saved you eight questions» without the list behind it is advertising.

What is not inherited is the conclusion. A clean check against the Regulation does not close the Annex A control: the standard asks for things the article does not, and the plan says so on every line.

Watching

They all prove the record was not altered. None proves the conclusion is still true

Digest of the whole repository14 / 14 superseded
Actaira: digest of the subject1 / 14 superseded
Expiry by control name0 / 14 superseded

Measured on the example repository, adding one line to datos/README.md. Reproducible with make fase8. The only control superseded is the Article 10 one, which is the only one that reads that data sheet. The other eleven do not read it, and that is why they still hold.

Evidence is superseded by the digest of the subject it was taken on, not by the name of that subject.

The digest of the whole repository paints a red wall every time somebody fixes a typo, and a red wall gets ignored within two weeks. Expiry by name supersedes nothing ever: it is a timer under another name.

The cost argument follows from the same place. Polling N repositories every minute is N × 1440 runs a day. Reacting to a push plus one expiry is on the order of how often you commit, plus one.

Honesty about scope

What Actaira does not do

It issues no compliance percentage

There is no such thing. What gets published is what was checked and what was not, with counts of obligations.

It does not say you comply

An obligation is met by an organisation, not by a control. It says what was checked by reading bytes, with which rule and by which author, and what falls outside the scope of that check.

It does not close ISO because the Regulation came out clean

A crosswalk says two texts talk about the same thing, not that meeting one meets the other. The standard asks for things the article does not.

It does not sign for you

Points 3 and 4 of Annex V are statements made by the provider under its sole responsibility. No program can make them, and no flag removes the word DRAFT.
